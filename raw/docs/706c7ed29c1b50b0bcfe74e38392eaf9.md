# PBL-H05 Teen Journalism Project PBL

## Level 1 (60 TOEFL)

**Session 1 — Journalism: The Art of Asking Questions**

**Objectives:**

* Introduce the class format

* Closeread a short excerpt (2-3 paragraphs) from a journalistic article: Analyze journalistic tone, the central question of the excerpt, and the types of evidence used

* Discuss what makes for a good journalistic question: open-ended ("what," "how," "why") and relatable

**Student Task:**

* Generate a list of two "big questions" related to topics like health, education, media, hobbies, identity, and family. (For example: "How do students at my school like to spend their free time?" "What do my classmates know about staying healthy?" "Why do siblings sometimes fight?"). If students are stuck, they can choose from a list of examples

**Session 2 — Interview Prep: Planning your Interview Questions**

**Objectives:**

* Choose a topic

* Learn how to conduct and take notes during an interview

* Create an interview note-taking worksheet, with at least 3 interview questions and at least 1 follow-up question

**Student Task:**

* Use interview worksheet to conduct two peer interviews

**Session 3 — Research: Finding your Experts**

**Objectives:**

* Understand how to find experts on your topic

* Read a short article / excerpt on the topic and pick out relevant information. If
applicable, note which sources are cited in the source. Note that level 1 students will have a source provided

* Emphasize the importance of a factual tone and recognize the difference between fact and opinion writing

**Student Task:**

* Preliminary outline worksheet

## Session 4 — Ledes: Making Readers Want to Read More

**Objectives:**

* Analyze successful ledes and headlines

* Introduce strong structure for headlines

* Introduce rules for strong journalistic tone: active voice, precise language

* Brainstorm ideas for an article lede

**Student Task:**

* Draft a 1 paragraph lede and one sentence or less headline

## Session 5 — Outlining: Finding the Shape of your Article

**Objectives:**

* Compare and identify connections between your interviews and expert source in order to understand 2-3 key points

* Understand three different structure types and choose the best fit for your topic: compare / contrast, pros / cons, problem / solution

* Identify and utilize transition phrases and contrast language ("while...," "however...", "despite...")

* Understand how (and when) to quote and how and when to paraphrase

**Student Task:**

* Draft your Article
**Session 6 — Revising: Making your Draft Great**

**Objectives:**

* Identify 2-3 key revision goals to work on in class
* Complete a checklist of requirements for a journalistic article: length, structure, use of sources, and style

**Student Task:**

* Finalize their article according to instructor feedback

# Level 2 (80 TOEFL)

**Session 1 — Intoducing Journalism: The Issues that Matter to You**

**Objectives:**

* Introduce the class format
* Discuss a short sample journalistic article (to be read before class), identifying the jouranlistic tone, structure, and types of evidence used
* Participate in a brainstorming activity to generate topic ideas related to health, education, media, hobbies, identity, or family

**Student Task:**

* Generate a list of three topic ideas, eached with a central investigatory question. (For example: "How do students at my school like to spend their free time?" "What do my classmates know about staying healthy?" "Why do siblings sometimes fight?").

**Session 2 — Interview Prep: Asking the Questions that Matter**

**Objectives:**

* Choose a topic
* Learn how to conduct and take notes during an interview
* Create a list of at least 3 interview questions and at least 2 follow-up questions to ask each interview subject

**Student Task:**

* Use interview worksheet to conduct three peer interviews
# Session 3 — Research: Finding Expert Sources

**Objectives:**

* Define expertise on the topic (for example, researchers, doctors, professors, etc.)

* Determine 1-2 research questions to guide the search of an expert source

* Learn how to use keyword sources to find an article that includes the perspective of experts and how to evaluate the reliability of a source

**Student Task:**

* Finish reading the source found in article and complete preliminary article outline

# Session 4 — Ledes and Headlines: Starting Strong

**Objectives:**

* Analyze successful ledes and headline

* Discuss types of ledes, including starting with a striking anecdote, a surprising piece of information, a question, or key pieces of information

* Brainstorm ideas for an article lede and headline

**Student Task:**

* Draft a 1-2 paragraph lede

# Session 5 — Finding Your Main Idea: Analyzing your Sources

**Objectives:**

* Compare and identify connections between your interviews and expert source

* Understand different structure types and choose the best fit for your topic or combining as needed: compare / contrast, pros / cons, problem / solution

* Identify 2-3 main points for the body paragraphs and organize evidence

* Discuss aspects of a strong analytical journalistic tone: active voice, concision, grammar

**Student Task:**

* Complete an outlining worksheet
**Session 6 — Revising: Identifying Points for Improvement**

**Objectives:**

* Identify 2-3 key revision goals to work on in class

* Complete a checklist of requirements for a journalistic article: main idea, structure, use of sources, and style

**Student Task:**

* Finalize their article according to instructor feedback

# Level 3 (100 TOEFL)

**Session 1 — Intoducing Journalism: Crafting your Pitch**

**Objectives:**

* Introduce the class format

* Discuss a full sample journalistic article (to be read before class), identifying the jouranlistic tone, structure, main idea, and use of evidence

* Introduce and analyze the structure of a professional journalistic pitch and discuss some of the possible outlets where journalists can pitch their work

**Student Task:**

* Create a pitch document, with a headline, main idea, research questions, preliminary structure, tone, and audience

**Session 2 — Interviews: Gathering Evidence from Stakeholders**

**Objectives:**

* Identify student stakeholders in your topic

* Learn how to conduct and take notes during an interview

* Create a question tree for interviews, with follow-up questions for various responses

**Student Task:**

* Complete question tree and conduct at least 3 peer interviews
# Session 3 — Research: Learning from Experts

**Objectives:**

* Define expertise on the topic (for example, researchers, doctors, professors, etc.)

* Introduce different types of evidence: background info, factual evidence, arguments and analysis

* Learn how to use keyword sources to find primary and secondary sources to support article

**Student Task:**

* Find 2 sources that incorporate the perspective of experts and write a summary of each, including key pieces of information, classifying the type of source, and evaluating reliablity and expertise

# Session 4 — Ledes: Capturing your Audience

**Objectives:**

* Close-read successful ledes, identifying how different ledes capture reader's attention and present the main idea of the article "in a nutshell"

* Discuss types of ledes, including starting with a striking anecdote, a surprising piece of information, a question, or key pieces of information

* Brainstorm ideas for an article lede, taking into account your audience, main idea, and purpose in writing

**Student Task:**

* Draft a 1-2 paragraph lede + outline worksheet

# Session 5 — Finding Your Main Idea: Analyzing your Sources

**Objectives:**

* Compare and identify connections between your interviews and expert source

* Understand different structure types and choose the best fit for your topic or combining as needed: compare / contrast, pros / cons, problem / solution

* Identify your purpose in wriitng and your audience

* Discuss aspects of a strong analytical journalistic tone: active voice, precision, vocabulary
**Student Task:**

* Complete an outline, including a "purpose statement"

**Session 6 — Revising: Your Best Work**

**Objectives:**

* Complete a checklist of requirements for a journalistic article: main idea, structure, use of sources, and style

* Evaluate how well each aspect of your article aligns with your purpose and audience

* Receive instructor feedback on structure, style, and content

**Student Task:**

* Finalize their article according to instructor feedback
