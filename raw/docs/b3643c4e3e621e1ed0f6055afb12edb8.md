# Statue of Liberty icon Urban Odyssey: Exploring NYC, Upstate New York & Long Island

## Project-Based Learning (PBL) Client Connection Guide (CCG)

# Question 1: What academic subjects does this course cover?

## Primary Learning Opportunities

This course is a **project-based academic writing and cultural exploration program** centered on real-world locations across **New York City, Upstate New York, and Long Island**. Students use geography, culture, history, and society as the foundation for structured academic writing.

**Students will explore multiple writing genres, including:**

* Travel writing

* Narrative storytelling

* Descriptive and reflective writing

* Research-based essays

* Analytical writing

* Creative writing

Through these genres, students develop core English academic writing skills such as structure, argument development, clarity, tone control, and evidence-based reasoning.

Students will analyze curated “Adventure Menus” featuring cities, boroughs, landmarks, historical sites, institutions, events, and cultural systems. They will transform observation into interpretation and then into structured written work.

## Interdisciplinary Learning Opportunities

In addition to English and writing, this course integrates multiple academic disciplines through real-world NYC and New York State contexts.
**Depending on the topic selected, students may explore:**

* **Geography & Urban Studies:** NYC boroughs, transportation systems, city planning, suburban vs. urban development (Long Island vs. NYC vs. Upstate NY)

* **History & Civics:** immigration, Ellis Island, industrial growth, political institutions, historical movements in New York State

* **Economics & Business:** Wall Street, major corporations, retail and service industries, tourism economies, regional economic differences

* **Cultural Studies & Media:** Broadway, film and TV representation of NYC, music scenes, sports culture, neighborhood identity

* **Environmental Science:** Hudson River Valley ecosystems, Adirondack conservation, coastal geography of Long Island

* **Sociology & Psychology:** urban identity, migration, community structures, inequality, cultural diversity in NYC neighborhoods

* **Journalism & Media Literacy:** analyzing news sources, writing location-based reports, interpreting cultural narratives in media

* **Creative Writing & Story Development:** students use real NYC and New York State locations as inspiration for fictional storytelling, character-driven narratives, and scene-based writing that blends imagination with real-world environments

# Question 2: What skills will students develop after completing the course?

## Writing Skills

* **Idea & Topic Development:** Students learn how to generate writing ideas from real-world places, cultural systems, historical contexts, and personal observations of NYC, Upstate New York, and Long Island. They are trained to turn broad themes into focused, structured writing topics.

* **Narrative Structure:** Students learn how to organize writing with clear progression (introduction → development → turning point → reflection → conclusion), applicable to both essays and storytelling formats.

* **Descriptive Writing:** Students use sensory detail and precise language to describe environments such as NYC neighborhoods, museums, bridges, stadiums, suburban towns, and natural landscapes.

* **Analytical Writing:** Students learn how to compare, interpret, and evaluate cultural, historical, and geographic systems across New York City and State.

* **Research Integration:** Students learn how to incorporate credible information from museums, government sources, academic institutions, and reputable publications into structured writing.
● **Argument Development**: Students practice forming clear claims supported by evidence drawn from real-world locations, events, and systems.

● **Revision & Editing**: Students develop the ability to refine clarity, structure, logic, and expression through guided multi-draft revision cycles.

## Research Skills

● **Targeted Research**: Students learn how to research NYC landmarks, borough histories, Upstate regions, Long Island communities, and cultural institutions using reliable academic and public sources.

● **Source Evaluation**: Students learn how to distinguish credible sources (museums, archives, universities, government data, established media) from informal or unreliable content.

● **Applied Research Writing**: Students learn how to integrate factual research into essays and narratives in a way that strengthens realism, depth, and academic credibility.

## Creative Storytelling Skills

● **Place-Based Storytelling**: Students learn how real-world locations in NYC and New York State can become the setting for fictional or semi-fictional stories, grounding imagination in real geography and culture.

● **Character Development in Real Settings**: Students create characters whose experiences are shaped by environments such as Manhattan streets, Brooklyn neighborhoods, Long Island suburbs, or Upstate towns.

● **Scene Building**: Students learn how to construct vivid, immersive scenes using real-world settings as narrative anchors.

● **Imaginative Expansion of Reality**: Students learn how to take real landmarks, events, and cultural spaces and build original storylines around them.

● **Perspective-Taking Through Fiction**: Students explore how different people might experience the same city or location differently, building empathy and narrative flexibility.

## Creative & Analytical Thinking Skills

● **Systems Thinking**: Students learn to view NYC and New York State as interconnected systems of geography, economy, culture, and infrastructure.

● **Comparative Thinking**: Students compare boroughs, regions, and environments (e.g., Manhattan vs. Brooklyn, NYC vs. Upstate NY, city vs. suburb).

● **Observation Skills**: Students learn to "read" environments like writers, journalists, and researchers, identifying meaningful details in real-world settings.
## Short-term benefits:

Improved academic writing, stronger structured expression, enhanced storytelling ability, and the ability to produce complete essays and narratives based on real-world contexts.

## Long-term benefits:

Students develop foundational skills that support:

* stronger school writing performance and GPA improvement

* improved standardized writing assessments

* stronger application essays for schools and summer programs

* enhanced interview communication and cultural fluency

* long-term academic confidence in humanities, social sciences, and writing-intensive fields

They also gain the ability to connect **real-world observation, academic reasoning, and creative storytelling into one unified skill set**, which is highly valuable for future academic and professional development.

## Question 3: Does this class have any prerequisites? What students are a good fit?

This course is designed for students in **Grades 5–10** and is fully adaptable based on English proficiency, writing experience, and academic readiness (TOEFL or equivalent). Instruction is personalized, meaning students at different levels can participate successfully through adjusted prompts, scaffolding, and individualized feedback.

Younger students (below Grade 5) may also be considered if they demonstrate strong readiness in English reading and writing, as well as consistent interest in storytelling, geography, or cultural exploration.

## Language Readiness (Recommended Benchmark)

**TOEFL 60+ (recommended baseline)**

Students should be able to read and produce basic English writing independently. However, placement is flexible and ultimately determined through writing samples
and a pretalk assessment rather than test scores alone. Students slightly below this range may still be admitted if they show strong engagement, motivation, and coachability.

# Question 4: What will students produce in this course?

## Single or Multi-Piece Porfolio

Students will complete a **multi-piece academic writing portfolio** built around NYC, Upstate New York, and Long Island. Depending on their level, interests, and pace, students may either:

* continue developing the **same writing piece over multiple weeks**, or

* create **new writing pieces each week across different genres and locations**

The course is designed to be flexible, allowing students to either **deeply revise and refine one major project over time** or build a **broader portfolio of multiple shorter and longer works**.

In some cases, students may also organize their work into a **themed collection (portfolio, travel writing guide, or narrative series)** that reflects their learning progression across the course.
Each final piece (or portfolio entry) will include:

* A clear academic or narrative introduction establishing the chosen NYC / Upstate NY / Long Island location or theme

* Structured body development, including description, storytelling, analysis, or research depending on genre

* Integration of real-world details and evidence connected to places, events, or cultural systems

* Creative or analytical depth showing either narrative development or critical interpretation

* A strong conclusion or reflection connecting writing to broader ideas such as identity, culture, or personal insight

* A fully revised final draft demonstrating improvement through multiple rounds of feedback

# Writing Process Structure (Key Feature of This Course)

Students will engage in a continuous writing and revision cycle, which may include:

* **Ongoing Development of the Same Piece:** A single essay or story may be refined over several weeks, gradually becoming more complex, detailed, and polished

* **Weekly New Writing Pieces:** Students may also choose to start fresh writing based on new NYC or New York State locations, themes, or prompts each week

* **Iterative Revision Cycles:** Every piece is expected to go through multiple drafts, not just one submission

* **Instructor-Guided Refinement:** Each revision includes targeted feedback focused on structure, clarity, depth, and expression

This structure ensures students are not only producing writing—but actively improving it over time.

# Writing Prompt Catalog (Core Idea Generation System)
This course includes a **comprehensive NYC, Upstate New York, and Long Island Writing Prompt Catalog** designed to help students continuously generate ideas across locations, themes, and genres.

The catalog serves as a structured idea bank that students can use throughout the course and beyond to:

* select writing topics

* explore new locations and landmarks

* connect real-world events to writing ideas

* develop creative stories, essays, and research topics

Students are encouraged to use the catalog not only for inspiration, but as a **writing decision-making tool** when choosing what to write each week.

***

Example categories included in the prompt catalog:

● **Locations & Boroughs**

Manhattan neighborhoods, Brooklyn communities, Queens cultural districts, Bronx historical sites, Staten Island natural areas, Upstate regions, and Long Island towns and coastal areas

● **Landmarks & Institutions**

Statue of Liberty, Central Park, Times Square, Wall Street, Yankee Stadium, Columbia University, Cooperstown Baseball Hall of Fame, Hudson River Valley sites, Hamptons communities, etc.

● **Events & Cultural Moments**

NYC Marathon, Fashion Week, Thanksgiving Parade, historical immigration waves, music festivals, sports championships, and regional fairs

● **People & Influencers**

Historical figures, political leaders, artists, musicians, athletes, business founders, and cultural icons connected to New York City and State

● **Media & Cultural References**

Films, TV shows, books, music, and journalism tied to NYC and surrounding regions

***

How students use it:

Students are guided to:

* pick a location or theme

* connect it to a genre (story, essay, research, reflection)

* and use the prompt catalog to generate structured writing ideas

**For example:**
* A student choosing **Brooklyn** → **identity** → **narrative story**
* A student choosing **Erie Canal** → **history** → **research essay**
* A student choosing **NYC music scene** → **cultural analysis** → **analytical essay**
